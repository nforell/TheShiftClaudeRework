How a shift works

Real shifts, compressed into twenty minutes.

The clock moves only when you act. Every assessment, med pass and phone call costs minutes, and your patients keep changing while you're busy.

01

Take report

The outgoing nurse talks you through every patient. Flag what matters for your brain sheet. Some of it is noise, and flagging everything counts against you.

02

Decide who's first

Rank your patients before you move. Nova, your charge nurse, tells you how her order compares, and explains why at the end.

03

Work the floor

Assess, give or hold meds, delegate, talk with patients and families, and call providers with SBAR. Tap anything in the room; the clues are there if you look.

04

See what your choices did

Outcomes for every patient, the decision that mattered most, and a timeline of each call you made, with the reasoning behind it.

What it trains

The judgment behind the checklist.

Built around the clinical judgment the Next Generation NCLEX tests: recognizing cues, prioritizing, acting, and evaluating what happened. Every debrief scores you across eight skills.

Patient safetyPrioritizationAssessmentMedication safetyDelegationCommunicationClinical judgmentTime management
  • No multiple choice. You decide what to look at, in what order, and what to do about it.
  • Consequences, not red X's. Give the wrong med and you find out when the patient does. Hold the right one and you'll see why.
  • Traps from real floors. The order that's active but unsafe. The family member being helpful. The report that says "probably a stomach bug."
  • A charge nurse in your pocket. Nova gives a few nudges a shift. She points; she won't answer for you.
Written by a working nurse, around the moments that actually go wrong on a shift, and the small things that catch them.
