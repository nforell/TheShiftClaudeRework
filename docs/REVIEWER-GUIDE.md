# Reviewing The Shift: a guide for clinical reviewers

Thank you for helping. The Shift is a nursing clinical-judgment game: you work a five-hour shift on a real unit, take report, decide who to see first, assess, give or hold meds, call providers with SBAR, and see what happens to your patients. Students and new grads use it to practice the thinking the Next Generation NCLEX tests. Before it launches, every shift needs a nurse who works in that specialty to check it.

## What we're asking

Check one shift (two if you like) against what a safe, current standard of care would teach a new nurse. You're not checking your hospital's specific policies. You're checking that nothing would teach a student something wrong or unsafe.

Time: about 20 minutes to play the shift, then about an hour on the review page. You can stop and come back; your answers save in your browser.

## Steps

1. **Play the shift once** at https://the-shift-claude-rework.vercel.app/play/. Pick your shift on the home screen. Play it the way you'd work it; the debrief at the end explains every scored decision.
2. **Open the review page** for that shift (link below). It lists every patient's history, report, labs, orders and meds, every scored decision with its points and the rationale players see, and the floor calls for that unit.
3. **Mark each item** *Accurate*, *Needs change* or *Not sure*. For *Needs change*, say what's wrong and what it should say. A reference helps but isn't required.
4. **Fill in your details** at the bottom: name, credentials, role and specialty, email, any references, anything else you noticed.
5. **Tick the sign-off box** if, with the changes you marked, the shift is accurate for nursing education. That box also gives permission to credit you by name in the game. Leave it unticked if you'd rather not be named, or aren't ready to sign off.
6. **Tap Send review.** If it opens your email app instead, send the email it fills in.

## What to look for

- **Doses, thresholds and timing**: hold parameters, protocol cutoffs, how fast something must happen.
- **Rationales**: is the reason given for each right or wrong call true, current and not overstated?
- **Scoring**: is anything rewarded that you'd call unsafe, or penalized that's reasonable practice?
- **Realism**: would this patient, order or call actually happen like this on your unit?
- **What's missing**: a cue a good nurse would catch that the shift ignores.

The adult and teen psych shifts use California law (5150, 5250, Riese, 5585); a setting in the game swaps in general US terms. Judge the clinical care, and flag the law only if it's wrong for California.

## The NCLEX practice questions

The game also has about 250 NCLEX-style questions and 19 unfolding case studies (home screen → NCLEX practice). Every question has a **Report a problem with this question** link at the bottom. If you try some, use it for anything wrong.
